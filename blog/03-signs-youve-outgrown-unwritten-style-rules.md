# Seven Signs Your Organization Has Outgrown Its "Unwritten" Style Rules

Every organization has style rules. The question is whether they're written down anywhere—or whether they only exist in the head of whoever has been there the longest.

Unwritten rules work fine at a small scale. They stop working the moment an organization grows past the point where everyone can just ask the same one or two people. Here's how to tell you've hit that point.

## 1. The same question keeps coming up in Slack or email

"Do we capitalize the program name?" "Is it 'board member' or 'Board Member'?" "How do we usually refer to our volunteers?" If you could set your watch by how often some version of this question resurfaces, that's the clearest sign of all. A style guide exists specifically to take that question out of the group chat.

## 2. New hires and volunteers take longer than they should to "sound right"

Every organization has a voice, even if it's never been written down. The trouble is that unwritten voice is only teachable by osmosis—new people learn it by having their drafts corrected, over and over, until they eventually absorb the pattern. That's a slow, frustrating way to onboard someone, for both sides.

## 3. Your content looks different depending on who wrote it

Pull up your last newsletter next to your last social post next to your last grant application. If you can tell they were written by three different people just from the tone and formatting—not the content, just the *style*—that's inconsistency your audience is picking up on too, even if they can't name it.

## 4. You've had a genuinely awkward public mistake

Referring to a donor by the wrong title in a public thank-you. Using an outdated term for the population you serve. Getting a program name wrong in a press release. These happen when there's no shared reference to catch it before publication—and they're exactly the kind of small, avoidable error that erodes trust faster than almost anything else.

## 5. Your organization has grown, but your communications team hasn't grown with it

More programs, more donors, more channels, more content—but still the same one or two people who "just know" how things should sound. That gap is where inconsistency creeps in fastest, because there's more surface area for it and no more capacity to catch it manually.

## 6. Freelancers and agencies keep missing the mark on tone

If every external writer's first draft needs heavy editing to "sound like us," the problem usually isn't the writer—it's that there's nothing for them to work from. A style guide is the single fastest way to get outside help producing on-brand content from the first draft.

## 7. You've never actually written any of this down

This is the quiet one. Plenty of organizations have a consistent style without realizing it—it's just living entirely in one or two people's heads. That's fine, right up until that person goes on leave, moves to a new role, or leaves the organization altogether, and the "rules" go with them.

## What this adds up to

None of these signs, on their own, is a crisis. Together, they're a pattern—and the pattern has a name: your organization has outgrown informal, word-of-mouth style, and it's time to write it down.

The good news is that this is one of the more straightforward gaps to close. It doesn't require new hires, new software, or a major overhaul. It requires one document, built once and revisited periodically, that turns "ask around and hope someone remembers" into "check the guide."
